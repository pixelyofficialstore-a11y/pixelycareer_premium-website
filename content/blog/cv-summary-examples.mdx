The summary is the first thing a recruiter reads after your name. Most people waste it.

> "A highly motivated, hardworking and dedicated individual seeking a challenging position in a well-reputed organization…"

That sentence could describe anyone. It tells the recruiter nothing about your field, your level or what you'd bring.

## The 3-line structure

1. **Who you are professionally:** your role or field, plus your level of experience.
2. **What you're good at:** two or three strengths that match the job you want.
3. **What you've done:** one concrete result or proof point.

## Examples you can adapt

**Customer service**
> Customer support specialist with 3 years in high-volume call centres. Skilled in complaint resolution, CRM systems and clear communication. Recognised for consistently high first-call resolution.

**Civil engineering**
> Civil engineer with 6 years in residential and commercial construction. Experienced in site supervision, quantity surveying and contractor coordination. Delivered multiple projects on schedule across two cities.

**Fresh graduate**
> Business graduate with internship experience in marketing and data reporting. Comfortable with Excel, social media analytics and presenting findings to managers. Ready to start in a marketing or business analyst role.

## Tailor it every time

Your summary is the easiest part of your CV to adjust for each application. Use the job title from the posting and mirror two or three of its key skills, as long as they're true for you.

<Callout type="tip" title="The swap test">
If you could paste your summary into a friend's CV and it would still fit, it's too generic.
</Callout>

## Words to cut

*Hardworking, motivated, dedicated, passionate, dynamic, results-oriented, team player.* Recruiters see them on almost every CV. Show those qualities through facts instead.

## The bottom line

Three lines: who you are, what you're good at, and one proof point. Specific beats impressive.
