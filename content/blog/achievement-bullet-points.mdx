"Responsible for managing sales." "Responsible for handling customers." Most CVs are full of lines like these. They describe the job. They don't describe *you*, and they don't tell a recruiter why you'd be better than the next applicant with the same title.

Achievement bullet points fix that.

## The formula

**Action + what you did + result.**

1. Start with a strong verb: *led, built, reduced, increased, resolved, launched, trained*.
2. Say what you did, specifically.
3. End with the result, and how you know it was a result.

<BeforeAfter
  before="Responsible for managing day-to-day sales operations."
  after="Managed daily showroom sales for a team of 6, consistently meeting monthly targets."
/>

## Examples by role

**Customer support**

<BeforeAfter
  before="Handled inbound and outbound customer calls."
  after="Handled 60+ customer calls a day, resolving most issues on the first call."
/>

**Engineering**

<BeforeAfter
  before="Worked on site supervision for construction projects."
  after="Supervised site work on 3 residential projects, delivering each within the planned schedule."
/>

**Accounts and finance**

<BeforeAfter
  before="Responsible for preparing monthly reports."
  after="Prepared monthly financial reports for senior management, cutting preparation time from 5 days to 3."
/>

**Teaching**

<BeforeAfter
  before="Taught mathematics to secondary students."
  after="Taught mathematics to 4 classes of 30+ students and introduced weekly practice tests that improved class averages."
/>

## No numbers? Use these instead

You don't need a spreadsheet to write a strong bullet. Think about:

- **Scale:** how many people, customers, projects or locations?
- **Frequency:** how often? Daily, weekly, every quarter?
- **Speed:** did something get faster?
- **Quality:** fewer errors, fewer complaints, better feedback?
- **Firsts:** did you set something up, train others, or get chosen for something?

<Callout type="note" title="Be honest">
Only include numbers you can explain in an interview. A believable "around 40 a week" is stronger than an impressive number you can't back up.
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

- **Starting every line with the same verb.** Vary them.
- **Listing tasks from the job description.** Recruiters already know what the job involves.
- **Writing paragraphs.** Keep each bullet to one or two lines.
- **Putting the best point last.** Lead with your strongest result.

## The bottom line

Go through your CV and rewrite every "Responsible for" line using action + what + result. It's the single biggest improvement most CVs can make.
