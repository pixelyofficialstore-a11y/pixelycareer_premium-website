Most resume templates are built to look good on Pinterest, not to be read by software. Applicant tracking systems (ATS) scan your resume before a recruiter does. If the software cannot read your sections, your experience may never be seen.

This template fixes that. It is plain on purpose. Clean structure is what gets you through.

## The template

Copy this order exactly:

1. **Name and contact line.** Name, phone, email, LinkedIn URL, city and state or country.
2. **Target job title.** The role you want, written the way job ads write it.
3. **Professional summary.** Three or four lines: who you are, your years of experience, two strengths and one result.
4. **Skills.** 8 to 12 hard skills taken from real job ads.
5. **Professional experience.** Job title, company, city, dates. Then 3 to 5 achievement bullets.
6. **Education.** Degree, school, year.
7. **Certifications.** Only the relevant ones.

That is the whole structure. Nothing more is required.

## Formatting rules that matter

<Callout type="tip" title="The five rules">
One column. Standard headings. A common font. No tables or text boxes. No important text inside the header or footer.
</Callout>

- **One column.** Two column layouts often get read in the wrong order.
- **Standard headings.** "Professional Experience" beats "My Journey". Software looks for known labels.
- **Common fonts.** Calibri, Arial, Helvetica or Garamond, size 10 to 12.
- **No graphics for text.** Skill bars, icons and logos carry no readable words.
- **Simple dates.** "Jan 2022 – Present" in the same format everywhere.

## How to fill each section

**Summary.** Write it last. Example:

<BeforeAfter
  before="Hardworking professional looking for a challenging role in a growing company."
  after="Project Manager with 7 years in SaaS and logistics. Delivered 14 projects worth $4.2M on schedule and cut budget overruns by 18%."
/>

**Experience bullets.** Start with a strong verb, show what you did, end with a result. Numbers help, but scale and frequency work too.

**Skills.** Open three job ads for your target role. Note the skills that repeat. Use the ones you genuinely have.

## Test it before you apply

Copy your resume and paste it into a plain text file. If the text comes out in the right order, with headings intact, an ATS can most likely read it too. If sections are jumbled, simplify the layout.

## The bottom line

A good ATS resume template is boring in the best way. One column, clear headings and strong content. The design gets you through the scan. The writing gets you the interview.
