Europass is a CV format created by the European Union to make qualifications and skills easier to compare across countries. You can build one on the official Europass website.

It's useful, but it isn't always the best choice.

## When you probably need Europass

- **The posting asks for it.** If an employer, university or programme says "Europass CV", use it.
- **EU-funded programmes and scholarships**, where a common format helps reviewers compare applicants.
- **Some public-sector and institutional roles** in Europe.

## When a regular CV may be better

- **Private-sector jobs** where no format is specified. A tailored, achievement-led CV often reads better.
- **Senior or creative roles**, where positioning and presentation matter more than a standard template.
- **Applications outside Europe.** Europass is rarely expected in the US, the UK private sector or the Gulf.

## Making a Europass CV stand out

The format is fixed, but the content is yours.

1. **Write a real summary.** Use the "About me" space for a short, specific professional summary.
2. **Show achievements**, not just duties, under each role.
3. **Be precise about languages.** Europass uses the CEFR scale (A1 to C2). Self-assess honestly.
4. **Keep it focused.** Leave out sections that don't help your application.
5. **Check the export.** Make sure the PDF reads cleanly.

## The bottom line

Use Europass when it's requested or expected. Otherwise, a strong tailored CV usually does a better job.
