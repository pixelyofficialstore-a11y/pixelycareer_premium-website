Recruiters search their applicant tracking system the way you search Google. If your resume does not use the words they type, it may not show up.

## Step 1: Collect five job ads

Find five ads for the exact role you want, in the country you are applying to. Copy the requirements sections into one document.

## Step 2: Mark what repeats

Highlight terms that appear in three or more ads. They usually fall into four groups:

- **Job titles:** Project Manager, Financial Analyst
- **Hard skills:** budgeting, forecasting, stakeholder management
- **Tools:** SAP, Power BI, Jira, Salesforce
- **Certifications:** PMP, CMA, CIPD

<Callout type="tip" title="Use the exact wording">
If ads say "financial modeling", don't write "building financial models" only. Use the term the recruiter will search.
</Callout>

## Step 3: Keep only what is true

Remove anything you cannot back up in an interview. Keywords open the door, but your experience has to hold it open.

## Step 4: Place them naturally

- **Summary:** your target title and two or three top skills
- **Skills section:** 8 to 12 core keywords
- **Bullets:** keywords inside real achievements

## Examples by role

| Role | Common keywords |
|---|---|
| Project Manager | Agile, Scrum, budget management, risk management, stakeholder management, PMP |
| Accountant | general ledger, reconciliation, month end close, IFRS, SAP, audit |
| Nurse | patient assessment, medication administration, BLS, ICU, care planning |
| Software Engineer | JavaScript, React, REST APIs, AWS, CI/CD, unit testing |
| Sales Executive | pipeline management, CRM, B2B sales, quota attainment, negotiation |

## Mistakes to avoid

- Stuffing the same keyword ten times
- Hiding keywords in white text
- Listing tools you have only heard of
- Using internal company jargon nobody searches for

## The bottom line

Your keywords should mirror the job ad and reflect your real experience. Find them in ads, keep the true ones, and weave them into results. That combination ranks well and reads well.
