Applying for jobs in the Gulf? The basics of a strong CV stay the same, but employers here look for a few extra details. Here is how the three biggest markets compare.

## Side by side

| | UAE | Saudi Arabia | Qatar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Photo | Common | Common | Common |
| Nationality | Usually listed | Important | Usually listed |
| Visa status | Often listed | Listed for expats | Often listed |
| Length | 2 pages | 2 pages | 2 pages |
| Main portals | LinkedIn, Bayt, Naukrigulf | LinkedIn, Bayt, Naukrigulf | LinkedIn, Bayt, Naukrigulf |

## What to include

- **Professional photo,** if you choose to add one: plain background, business clothing
- **Nationality,** since many roles consider visa and quota requirements
- **Current location and visa status,** for example "Dubai, UAE · Employment visa, transferable"
- **Notice period or availability**
- **Driving license,** if relevant to the role
- **Languages,** with level

## What to leave out

- Religion
- Father's name
- Passport number
- Full date of birth (age is optional)
- Skill bars and hobby lists

## Market notes

**UAE.** A very international market. Strong English, results and clear visa status help you stand out.

**Saudi Arabia.** Many roles are linked to Saudization targets, so nationality matters. Professional registrations such as the Saudi Council of Engineers add credibility.

**Qatar.** Large infrastructure, energy and hospitality sectors. Relevant certifications and Gulf experience carry weight.

<Callout type="tip" title="ATS still applies">
Bayt, Naukrigulf and most company portals use applicant tracking systems. Keep one column and standard headings, even with a photo.
</Callout>

## The bottom line

A Gulf CV is a clean ATS CV with a few regional details added. Include what employers use to make decisions, leave out personal information they don't need, and show results in numbers and local currency where you can.
