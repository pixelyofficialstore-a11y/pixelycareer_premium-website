Most job applications today go through software before they reach a person. That software is called an **applicant tracking system**, or ATS. An ATS CV is simply a CV built so that software can read it correctly, and so the recruiter who opens it wants to keep reading.

That's it. There's no magic score and no secret template. There are two jobs: be readable, then be convincing.

## What an ATS actually does

When you apply online, the ATS stores your application and turns your CV into searchable text. Recruiters then search, filter and sort candidates, often by job title, skills and experience.

If your CV can't be read cleanly, parts of it may end up in the wrong place or disappear. If it doesn't use the words the recruiter searches for, it may not show up near the top. Either way, a strong candidate can quietly get missed.

## The two parts of an ATS CV

### 1. A format software can read

- **One column.** Text flows top to bottom in the order a person would read it.
- **Real text.** No text inside images, logos or graphics.
- **Standard headings.** "Experience", "Education", "Skills". Not "My Journey".
- **Simple formatting.** Clear dates, plain bullet points, no tables for layout.
- **A clean file.** Usually Word or a text-based PDF, unless the job posting asks for something else.

### 2. Content a recruiter wants to read

- **A summary written for the role** you're applying for, not a general description of yourself.
- **Keywords from real job postings**: the job titles, skills and tools employers actually list.
- **Achievement-led bullet points** that show results, not just duties.

## Check your CV in 10 minutes

1. **The copy-paste test.** Open your CV, select all the text and paste it into a plain text editor. Does it read in the right order? Are your name, job titles and dates still there? If it's scrambled, software may struggle too.
2. **The heading test.** Are your sections named in plain words a stranger would recognise?
3. **The keyword test.** Open two job postings for the role you want. Highlight the skills and job titles they repeat. How many of those appear in your CV, in the same words?
4. **The bullet test.** Count your bullets that start with "Responsible for". Each one is a chance to show a result instead.
5. **The six-second test.** Give your CV to someone for six seconds. Can they tell you what job you want and why you're a fit?

<Callout type="tip" title="A quick rule">
If a recruiter couldn't find your target job title in your CV, assume the software can't either.
</Callout>

## What an ATS CV is not

- **It's not a plain, boring document.** Clean layout and good typography still matter to the human reader.
- **It's not keyword stuffing.** Repeating terms or hiding them in white text doesn't help and looks bad to recruiters.
- **It's not a guarantee.** A good ATS CV makes sure you're read and taken seriously. Hiring decisions are still made by people.

## The bottom line

An ATS CV is a clear, single-column CV with the right words in the right places, and results that make a recruiter stop. If yours fails the copy-paste or keyword test, that's the first thing to fix.
