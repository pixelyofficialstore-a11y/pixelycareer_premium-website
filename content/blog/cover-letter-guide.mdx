Many cover letters start with "I am writing to apply for the position of…" and then repeat the CV in paragraph form. Recruiters stop reading.

A good cover letter does something your CV can't: it connects your experience to *this* job and *this* employer, in your own voice.

## The 4-part structure

### 1. The opening: why this role

Name the role and give one specific reason you're interested, or one strong fact about you.

> "I'm applying for the Senior Accountant role at Al Noor Group. I've spent five years managing month-end close for multi-entity businesses, and your expansion across the GCC is exactly the kind of challenge I'm looking for."

### 2. The proof: two achievements

Pick two achievements from your CV that match the job's main requirements. Tell them briefly, with results.

### 3. The fit: why you and why them

Show you understand what the employer needs. Mention something specific: a project, a value, a market they're entering.

### 4. The close: a clear next step

> "I'd welcome the chance to discuss how I can support your finance team. Thank you for your time."

## Mistakes that get letters skipped

- **Starting with "To whom it may concern"** when you could find a name or use "Dear Hiring Team".
- **Repeating your CV** line by line.
- **Writing about what you want** instead of what you'll bring.
- **Sending the same letter everywhere.** Recruiters can tell.
- **Going over one page.**

<Callout type="tip" title="Reuse, but adapt">
Write one strong base letter, then change the opening, the two achievements and the "why them" paragraph for each application.
</Callout>

## Country notes

- **US and UK:** keep it concise and professional. UK letters use UK spelling.
- **Gulf:** it's common to mention your current location, visa status and availability near the end.
- **Europe:** if the employer asks for a motivation letter, focus more on why you want the role and the organisation.

## The bottom line

One page, four parts: why this role, two pieces of proof, why you fit, and a clear close. Specific always beats long.
