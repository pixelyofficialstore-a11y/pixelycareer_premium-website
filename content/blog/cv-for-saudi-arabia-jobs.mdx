Saudi Arabia's job market has changed a lot in recent years, with new sectors, large projects and growing demand for skilled professionals. Recruiters in Riyadh, Jeddah and Dammam want CVs that are easy to scan and clearly matched to the role.

As always, these are **common practices**. The job posting comes first.

## Format

- **Single column** with clear headings and real text, so applicant tracking systems can read it.
- **Around two pages** for most professionals.
- **Consistent dates** in month and year format.

## Personal details

- Full name, phone with country code, email and current city
- **Nationality**, and **Iqama or visa status** if you're already in the Kingdom
- **Availability** or notice period
- A professional photo, if you choose to include one

## Content

- **A targeted summary** with your role, years of experience and main strengths.
- **Relevant regional experience**: GCC employers, projects or clients.
- **Achievement-led bullet points** showing scale and results.
- **Certifications and licences** clearly listed, for example engineering or professional body memberships.
- **Keywords** from Saudi job postings for your role.

<Callout type="tip" title="English or Arabic?">
An English CV works for many private-sector roles. If you're targeting government or semi-government employers, check whether an Arabic version is requested.
</Callout>

## Job profiles

Recruiters often search **LinkedIn**, **Indeed** and **Naukrigulf**. Make sure your profiles match your CV in job titles, dates and achievements.

## Checklist before you apply

1. Target role at the top
2. Contact details with country code
3. Nationality, Iqama or visa status, and availability
4. Single-column layout that reads cleanly as plain text
5. Certifications listed clearly
6. Keywords from the posting
7. Job profiles updated to match

## The bottom line

Make it easy for a Saudi recruiter to see who you are, whether you can join, and what you've delivered.
