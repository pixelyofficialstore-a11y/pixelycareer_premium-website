Open any template website and most of the designs you'll see have two columns: a coloured sidebar with a photo, contact details and skill bars, and the main content on the right. They look polished. They're also one of the most common reasons a good CV underperforms.

## Why two columns cause problems

### For hiring software

Applicant tracking systems read your CV as text. With two columns, the software has to guess the reading order. Some systems read straight across both columns, mixing your skills list into your job history. Others skip the sidebar entirely. Either way, information can end up scrambled or missing.

### For recruiters

Recruiters skim. Their eyes go down the page looking for your job titles, employers, dates and results. A sidebar splits that attention, and the narrower main column pushes your experience onto more pages.

### For your content

Sidebars encourage filler: skill bars with percentages, icons, and lists like "Hardworking" and "Team player". These take up space but tell a recruiter very little.

## Side by side

| | One column | Two columns |
|---|---|---|
| Read correctly by software | Reliably | Sometimes |
| Easy to skim | Yes | Harder |
| Space for achievements | More | Less |
| Looks modern | Yes, with good typography | Yes |

## When a two-column design can work

- **You're handing it over in person**, for example at a networking event.
- **The employer explicitly wants a designed CV**, as in some creative roles.
- **You also have an ATS version** to submit through online systems.

In those cases, keep both: a designed version for people and a single-column version for online applications.

<Callout type="tip" title="Not sure which one you have?">
Copy all the text from your CV and paste it into a plain text editor. If your skills appear in the middle of your work history, the layout is working against you.
</Callout>

## How to make one column look premium

- Use one clean font and two or three sizes.
- Put your name and target role at the top, large and clear.
- Use thin rules or a single accent colour for section headings.
- Leave white space. It makes a page look confident, not empty.

## The bottom line

For online applications, choose one column. Make it look professional with typography and spacing, not with sidebars and graphics.
