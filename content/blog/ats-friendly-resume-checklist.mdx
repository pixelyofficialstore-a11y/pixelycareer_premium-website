An ATS friendly resume is one that software can read correctly and a recruiter can skim in seconds. Use this checklist before every application.

## Format

1. **Single column layout.** Two columns can scramble the reading order.
2. **No tables, text boxes or columns made with tabs.** They often hide content from the parser.
3. **Contact details in the main body,** not only in the header area.
4. **Standard section headings:** Summary, Skills, Professional Experience, Education, Certifications.
5. **A common font** at 10 to 12 points.
6. **Consistent dates** in one format, for example "Mar 2021 – Present".

<Callout type="note" title="Why format comes first">
If the system cannot read a section, the best keywords in that section count for nothing.
</Callout>

## Content

7. **Your target job title near the top,** matching the wording used in job ads.
8. **Keywords from the job ad,** placed naturally in your summary, skills and bullets.
9. **Both versions of key terms** where relevant, such as "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)".
10. **Achievement bullets,** not duty lists.

<BeforeAfter
  before="Responsible for social media accounts."
  after="Grew Instagram followers from 4K to 22K in 8 months with a weekly content calendar."
/>

## File

11. **PDF, unless the job asks for Word.** Name it clearly: Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf.
12. **Run the plain text test.** Paste the whole resume into a text editor. If headings and sections appear in order, you are ready.

## Common traps

- Skill bars and star ratings. Software reads nothing from them.
- Creative headings like "Where I've Been".
- Important information inside images or icons.
- Keyword stuffing in white text. Recruiters notice, and it damages trust.

## The bottom line

Run through all 12 points once and fix what fails. Most resumes only need small changes to read cleanly. Then spend your time on what really wins interviews: strong, specific bullets.
