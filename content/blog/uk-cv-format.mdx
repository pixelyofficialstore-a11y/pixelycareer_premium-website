UK recruiters expect a clear, two page CV that gets to the point quickly. Here is the format they are used to seeing.

## The structure

1. **Name and contact details:** phone, email, LinkedIn, town or city
2. **Personal statement:** 3 to 5 lines about who you are and what you offer
3. **Key skills:** 6 to 10 relevant skills
4. **Work experience:** most recent first, with achievements
5. **Education**
6. **Certifications or training** if relevant

## What to leave out

UK employers try to avoid bias, so leave out:

- Photo
- Date of birth or age
- Marital status
- Nationality, unless relevant to the right to work
- Full home address (town or city is enough)

<Callout type="note" title="Right to work">
If you need sponsorship or already have the right to work in the UK, a short line such as "Full right to work in the UK" helps recruiters.
</Callout>

## Writing the personal statement

Keep it specific, not generic.

<BeforeAfter
  before="A hardworking and motivated individual looking for a challenging position."
  after="CIPD qualified HR Advisor with 5 years in retail and logistics, supporting teams of up to 400 and reducing staff turnover by 12%."
/>

## Style tips

- **UK spelling:** organisation, optimise, programme
- **Dates:** "Jan 2022 – Present"
- **Length:** two pages maximum for most people
- **Tone:** confident and factual, without exaggeration
- **References:** "Available on request" is optional and can be left out

## UK CV vs Europass

The UK uses its own CV style. Europass is mainly used for EU applications and some education programmes. If you are applying to both, keep two versions.

## The bottom line

A strong UK CV is short, factual and focused on results. Leave out personal details, use UK spelling, and open with a personal statement that tells the recruiter exactly what you bring.
