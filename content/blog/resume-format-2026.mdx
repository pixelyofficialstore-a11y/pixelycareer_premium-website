There are three resume formats. One works for almost everyone, one works for a few, and one usually backfires.

## 1. Reverse chronological

Your most recent job first, then the one before it.

**Best for:** most professionals, steady career paths, anyone applying through online portals.

**Why it works:** recruiters see your current level instantly, and ATS systems read it cleanly.

## 2. Combination (hybrid)

A strong skills summary at the top, followed by your experience in reverse chronological order.

**Best for:** career changers, people returning after a break, and specialists with many transferable skills.

**Why it works:** it leads with what you can do, but still shows your history clearly.

## 3. Functional

Skills grouped by theme, with job history reduced to a short list or removed.

**Best for:** very few people.

**Why to avoid it:** recruiters often read it as a way to hide gaps. Software struggles to connect skills to roles.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick decision">
Steady career? Chronological. Changing careers or returning after a gap? Combination. Functional? Almost never.
</Callout>

## What a 2026 resume looks like

- One column, clean headings, lots of white space
- A target job title under your name
- A three or four line summary with one real result
- 8 to 12 skills that match job ads
- Achievement bullets with numbers where possible
- One page for early careers, two pages for experienced professionals

## Country differences

The format stays the same, but the details change:

- **US:** no photo, no date of birth, called a resume.
- **UK:** usually no photo, up to two pages, called a CV.
- **Gulf:** a professional photo is common, and nationality and visa status are often listed.

## The bottom line

Choose reverse chronological unless you have a clear reason not to. Then spend your effort on content. Format gets you read. Results get you shortlisted.
