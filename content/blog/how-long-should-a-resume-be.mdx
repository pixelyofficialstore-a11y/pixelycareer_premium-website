The honest answer: as long as it needs to be, and not one line longer. Here are the simple rules most recruiters follow.

## By experience level

| Experience | Length |
|---|---|
| Student or graduate | 1 page |
| 1 to 5 years | 1 page, 2 if very relevant |
| 5 to 15 years | 2 pages |
| Senior or executive | 2 pages, 3 only if needed |

## By country

- **US:** one page is preferred for early careers. Two pages is fine for experienced roles.
- **UK:** two pages is the standard for most professionals.
- **Gulf:** two pages is common, especially with a photo and personal details.

<Callout type="tip" title="The first page matters most">
Recruiters often decide on page one. Put your summary, skills and most recent role there.
</Callout>

## What to cut first

1. Jobs older than 10 to 15 years, unless highly relevant
2. Duties that repeat across roles
3. Soft skills without proof
4. "References available on request"
5. Hobbies, unless they relate to the job

## How to make it fit

- Keep bullets to two lines each
- Use 10 to 11 point font, not smaller
- Reduce margins slightly, but keep white space
- Combine older roles under "Earlier Experience"

## The bottom line

Length is about relevance, not rules. If every line helps you get the job, two pages is perfect. If you're padding to fill space, one strong page wins.
