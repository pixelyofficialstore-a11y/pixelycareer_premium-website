If you apply in more than one country, you'll quickly notice that "CV" and "resume" don't always mean the same thing, and neither do the expectations behind them.

The guidance below describes **common practice**. Employers and industries vary, so the job posting always wins.

## The short version

| | United States | United Kingdom | Gulf (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Usual word | Resume | CV | CV |
| Typical length | 1–2 pages | Around 2 pages | 2 pages, sometimes more for senior roles |
| Photo | Usually left out | Usually left out | Often included |
| Personal details | Name, contact, location | Name, contact, location | Often adds nationality and visa status |
| Spelling | US English | UK English | Either, used consistently |

## United States: resume

In the US, "resume" is the standard word for a job application document. A "CV" usually means a longer academic or research document listing publications and teaching.

US resumes tend to be concise and results-focused. Photos, date of birth and marital status are usually left out.

## United Kingdom: CV

In the UK, "CV" is the everyday word. Around two pages is common. Photos and date of birth are usually left out, and UK spelling is expected (*organised*, *programme*, *colour*).

For applications to EU employers or programmes, you may be asked for a **Europass CV**, the EU's standard format.

## The Gulf: CV

In the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Qatar, "CV" is the usual term. Many applicants include a professional photo, nationality and current visa status, because employers often consider these when hiring internationally. Practice varies, so check what each posting asks for.

Keywords matter here too: many Gulf employers and job boards use applicant tracking systems.

<Callout type="note" title="What stays the same everywhere">
A clean, readable layout. A summary written for the role. Achievement-led bullet points. Keywords from real job postings. These work in every market.
</Callout>

## Applying to more than one country?

Keep one master document with your full history, then create a version for each market: adjust the length, spelling, personal details and keywords to match.

## The bottom line

The name changes and some details change, but the fundamentals don't. Adapt the format to the market and keep the content focused on results.
